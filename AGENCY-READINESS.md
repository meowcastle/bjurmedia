# Bjur Media — Agency-Readiness Roadmap (12 months)

Goal: move from solopreneur to a shop that can credibly bid, win, and survive
enterprise work (Levi's-tier brands, major music labels) — without losing the
speed, taste, and retainer floor that make Bjur dangerous in the first place.

Positioning target: **director-led boutique + embedded content partner with an
attribution loop no one else has.** Sell the system, not the shoots.

---

## Q1 — Pass procurement (make Bjur safe to hire)

These are binary gates. None make you better; all make you *possible*.

- [ ] **Entity & books**: confirm LLC standing, separate business banking,
      clean P&L (an accountant if not already). Consider S-corp election with
      the accountant once enterprise revenue is realistic.
- [ ] **Insurance package** (~$1–2k/yr ongoing + per-project riders):
  - [ ] General liability $1M/$2M (table stakes on every COI request)
  - [ ] Workers' comp (required the moment crew is on set — even 1099 crew
        in NY; verify with broker)
  - [ ] Commercial auto / hired & non-owned (rentals, gear runs)
  - [ ] Umbrella $1–5M (big brands often require above GL)
  - [ ] E&O (needed for anything with distribution; labels ask)
  - Broker tip: use a film/production-specialty broker (Wrapbook, Athos,
    Front Row), not a generic small-biz one. Per-project policies from ~$400
    cover one-offs before the annual policy pencils out.
- [ ] **Contract kit** (one lawyer session, ~$1–2.5k):
  - [ ] Own MSA + SOW template (so smaller clients sign *your* paper)
  - [ ] Redline literacy for *their* paper: indemnification, IP assignment,
        insurance riders, payment terms — know your walk-away lines
  - [ ] Crew deal memo + talent/appearance release templates
  - [ ] Music licensing rider (labels care; brands' legal teams care more)
- [ ] **Cash flow armor**: enterprise means net 60–90 while you front crew,
      rentals, locations for weeks.
  - [ ] Business line of credit or invoice-financing relationship set up
        *before* you need it
  - [ ] Target reserve: enough to carry one mid-size job (~$50–80k of
        fronted costs) without stress
  - [ ] Standard terms in your bids: 50% on award / 50% net 30 where you
        have leverage; know your minimum acceptable when you don't

## Q2 — Build capacity (kill the bus factor)

The enterprise question is never "is the work good," it's "what breaks if
Justin is unavailable." Answer it structurally.

- [ ] **Bench**: identify and repeat-book a core roster — 2 DPs, 2 editors,
      1 producer, 1 sound, 1 colorist. The bar: people you'd trust on set
      with a client and no Justin. Crew the same people across 2–3 jobs so
      the unit is proven, not theoretical.
- [ ] **Payroll/onboarding mechanism** for crew (Wrapbook or similar): deal
      memos, COI tracking, W-9s, timecards. This is also what lets you
      absorb *their* vendor paperwork instead of it absorbing you.
- [ ] **Fractional EP**: one experienced commercial EP relationship —
      per-project is fine. They run AICP bids, agency relationships, and
      set logistics; you direct. This single hire changes what tier of work
      you can credibly pitch.
- [ ] **AICP bid capability**: EP builds the first one; you learn the
      format (firm bid vs cost-plus, markup norms, insurance line). If you
      can't produce an AICP budget in 48 hours, you're not in the triple-bid
      game agencies run.
- [ ] **Rate architecture**: publish (internally) a real rate card — your
      director day rate, unit day rates by configuration, post rates,
      licensing schedule. Margin comes from selling the unit, not your day.

## Q3 — Build proof (make the case undeniable)

- [ ] **Case studies from portal data** — your unfair advantage. The portal
      already ties delivered assets to IG/YT view performance. Turn 2–3
      client stories into one-pagers: volume delivered, publish cadence,
      view curves per asset, what the retainer system replaced. No other
      shop this size can show attribution; most can't show anything.
- [ ] **Capabilities deck** built around three pillars: director-led craft
      (reel), the embedded-partner system (portal, calendar, Slack loop,
      insights), and production capacity (bench, insurance, bid-ready).
- [ ] **Site refresh**: bjurmedia.nyc should read "boutique production
      company," not "freelancer" — work-first, director credit, the system
      as a named offering (worth branding the portal/loop as a product,
      e.g. "the Bjur delivery system").
- [ ] **Portal licensing → enterprise vocabulary**: reframe SOCIAL /
      COMMERCIAL / BUYOUT tiers into the four dials enterprise buyers
      negotiate — **term** (months/perpetuity), **territory**, **media
      channels** (organic social / paid social / OOH / broadcast), and
      **exclusivity**. Rule-of-thumb market rates: usage adds 20–100% of
      the content fee; paid-media usage prices at the top; full buyout
      2–3x. The License model already freezes scope text — extend the
      schema (term, territory, channels, exclusivity fields) when a deal
      needs it, not before.
- [ ] **One flagship spec or passion piece** aimed at the tier you want
      (culture/music adjacent) — the treatment-quality sample that label
      commissioners and agency creatives actually respond to.

## Q4 — Build pipeline (get into the rooms)

Nobody cold-pitches Levi's corporate. The routes:

- [ ] **Agency producers**: the people who assemble triple-bids at Levi's
      (and peers') agencies. Target: be one of the three names an agency
      producer thinks of for culture-forward NYC work. Motion: EP intros,
      producer-community events, sending the deck to heads of production —
      not creatives.
- [ ] **Brand culture programs**: Levi's-tier brands run music/culture
      marketing programs with separate budgets and looser procurement than
      national campaigns. Regional and seasonal activations are the entry
      door; the community work (57.NYC etc.) is the credential — it's the
      authenticity these programs are buying.
- [ ] **Label side**: video commissioners + artist managers buy via
      director treatments. Build a treatment template you can turn around
      fast; artist/tour content retainers are the same retainer muscle you
      already run, at label budgets.
- [ ] **In-house studio overflow**: brands with internal studios (the
      Nike/Sephora/Red Bull pattern) run hybrid — velocity content
      in-house, craft + overflow external. Pitch the embedded-partner
      system directly at in-house studio leads; your portal is literally
      built for that relationship shape.
- [ ] **Revenue mix guardrail**: community retainers stay as the floor
      (they fund the runway and are the brand story); enterprise projects
      layer on top. Don't trade the moat for one logo.

## Readiness scorecard (check quarterly)

| Signal | Solopreneur | Agency-level |
|---|---|---|
| COI request | scramble | same-day PDF |
| "Who's your team?" | "me + freelancers" | named unit, 3 jobs together |
| AICP bid request | pass | 48-hr turnaround via EP |
| Net-90 PO | cash crisis | financed, priced in |
| Usage rights ask | flat tier | term/territory/channel/exclusivity schedule |
| "Show me results" | reel | reel + per-asset attribution data |
| Justin unavailable | everything stops | unit ships, Justin directs |

## Rough budget for the year

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Insurance package | $1.5–3k/yr + ~$400–1k per project |
| Legal (MSA kit, redline session) | $1.5–3k one-time |
| Fractional EP | per-project (built into bids) |
| Payroll platform | per-job fees, pass-through |
| Site/deck refresh | sweat equity + design budget as needed |
| Line of credit | $0 until drawn; set up while you don't need it |

Total cash outlay before the first enterprise job: **~$5–8k.** The gates are
cheap. The discipline is the expensive part.
